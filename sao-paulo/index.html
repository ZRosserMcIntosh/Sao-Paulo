Street level

The city you already know

MASP floating over Avenida Paulista, the canopy of Jardins, the towers of Faria Lima. Buildings come from GeoSampa's 3D footprints and OpenStreetMap; sidewalks, curbs, trees, poles and bins are placed from the city's own survey layers, then checked automatically.

Underground

The Metrô, at its real depth

Fourteen lines run through both blocks in their own geometry layer (Metrô, CPTM trains, the airport express and the Line 17 monorail): tunnels, viaducts, 103 station stops and 169 street entrances. Depths come from published figures where they exist (Line 6's Itaberaba–Hospital Vila Penteado sits 65.7 m down) and line-typical values elsewhere. The stations are walkable: real stairs, escalators, fare lines and platforms. The plan below shows block 1; the full network is on the map's Metrô layer.

Inside the stations

Ten stations, rendered

Path-traced renders of the walkable station interiors as they stand in the game world: platforms, screen doors, tactile strips, line-colour bands and name plates, lit by their own ceiling strips. Materials are procedural (tile panels, granite floors, cast concrete); textures from real photos come later.

Landmarks

Built by hand where it matters

A few structures get their own models on top of the procedural city: the Octávio Frias de Oliveira cable-stayed bridge, Estação da Luz with its clock tower and glazed train shed, the airport at Congonhas, the Line 17 monorail.

Every street, named

Turn a corner, see the name

Street names are matched between OpenStreetMap and the city's official street register, down to each block's house numbers. In the game, the name and neighbourhood fade in when you turn onto a street. At every crossing, the city's blue name plates stand on the corner.

Quality

Block 1: 576 tiles, checked by machine

Every 500 m tile is opened headless and inspected for objects standing in the road, floating buildings, terrain poking through asphalt, missing bridges and seams with its neighbours. Then eight automatic repair passes run, and the tile is checked again. The goal is every tile at grade A: deep green. Block 1 got there on 13 October; block 2 goes through the same passes next.

Repair passes
  1. Street furniture: duplicates removed, bins and trees moved out of the carriageway
  2. Lane paint re-seated on the asphalt; building bases dropped to the ground on slopes
  3. Road surface smoothed; terrain carved under every street
  4. Poles off the road, outdated 2014 footprints hidden, gaps in the street network filled
  5. Level-aware smoothing so viaducts don't average with the streets under them
  6. Bridge decks for the Minhocão and the river bridges; floating towers grounded
  7. Streets re-fitted along their length so they are level across, as real streets are
  8. The last steep steps flattened, ramp skirts turned into retaining walls, paint re-laid, objects moved off the curb line
What comes next

Four times the city

The plan is to grow the 24 × 24 grid to 48 × 48 tiles, 24 km on a side. Two of the four blocks are built: the original map and the north block (Santana, Casa Verde, Tremembé). The east block (Mooca, Tatuapé, Vila Prudente) is being built now, and the north-east (Vila Maria, Penha, the edge of Guarulhos) follows.

Tiles inside the city use the same GeoSampa survey layers as today. Tiles outside it (São Caetano, Santo André, Guarulhos) will be built from OpenStreetMap alone, with a coarser terrain model.

Santa Catarina · the second city

Balneário Camboriú

The same pipeline, pointed 600 km south at Brazil's tallest skyline. 116 tiles from the Barra Sul jetty to Praia Brava, built from the city's own pseudo-3D cadastre: every building carries a height and a confidence flag, every street its name, house-number range and CEP. It is the second of four 1:1 cities the road network will join.

Heights come from the cadastre's 3D units (checked against OpenStreetMap floor counts), from the CTBUH list for the named towers, or are flagged as provisional. Cross streets are named by number and houses numbered by the metre, so the CEP ranges from Correios fall into place along each axis.

Headed for Unreal Engine 5

Hit a pole, it falls

Every object carries a crash class for the game's physics: lamp posts snap at 35 km/h, concrete distribution poles at 60, thick trees stop a car cold, and saplings bend out of the way. Street names stream with each tile, so the banner works anywhere in the map.
