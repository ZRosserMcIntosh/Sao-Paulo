How the Pole Lab works

The street is six 11 m concrete double-T poles 35 m apart, the kind that line São Paulo's residential streets. Each pole carries three 13.8 kV conductors on a crossarm, four low-voltage conductors, a telecom bundle and service drops to the houses. Whether a pole has a transformer or a streetlight comes from its position, exactly as the game's world builder decides it.

Everything you see is simulated by Pole Dynamics, the game's native C++ module, compiled to WebAssembly. The page only draws it. It is the same code, with the same results bit for bit, that runs in the Unity build ("Replay & compare" shows the state hash).

What happens in a hit

  • The pulse. The car's front crushes against the pole (about 400 kN/m) for 20–60 ms. The lab reports the peak force, the impulse, how much speed the car loses, and the pulse length.
  • The pole. The force at the bumper is checked against the section's shear strength, and the moment at the ground line against its bending strength. A hit can crack the pole (a permanent tilt), lean it in the soil, or snap it at the bumper. The soil is an elastic–plastic spring; wet ground is softer and weaker.
  • The wires. Spans hang on their analytic catenary until something disturbs them. Then they wake up as ropes of 10–24 nodes, solved with XPBD at 240 Hz. Ties and clamps let go, conductors snap and whip back, and wires fall onto the road.
  • Hazards. A live 13.8 kV conductor on the ground marks a 4 m danger zone and arcs. Lanes with anything below 2.2 m are reported blocked. Houses and streetlights go dark when their transformer's section loses power.
  • The transformer. It tears off its brackets when the pulse shakes the tank above about 4 g, and falls as its own body. A hard landing raises oil-leak and fire hooks.

What it does not do

At default settings, neighbouring poles are pulled out of plumb but do not fall over: the wires' ties and clamps let go first, which is what distribution lines are designed to do. The "Domino" scenario turns the tunables to art-directed values to show a chain. Flat ground, rigid pole pieces and one global wetness are other simplifications. The module's README lists them all, with sources for every number.

A game simulation, not engineering software: do not use it to assess real poles or power lines. Stay well away from downed wires in real life and call the utility.
