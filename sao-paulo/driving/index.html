Car

Showing the placeholder body. Drop a .glb exported from Blender anywhere on the page to drive it.

Sound

Synthesized flat-plane V8 (no recordings): every firing pulse is generated from the engine rpm and load, with tyre squeal from the physics' slip, wind and road noise. The Bonnet camera hears it from the cabin.

Driving preset

Presets only change the driver aids. Grip, power and weight are identical in all three.

Aids

Drifting: pick Drift (4); it fits the drift kit (52° lock, tight LSD). Turn in hard on the throttle and the assist kicks the clutch so the rear steps out (or use the handbrake), keep the throttle in, then steer where you want to go: hold the steering into the corner to keep drifting (more steering, more angle and a tighter line) and let go once the car points down the road to straighten out. The drift assist countersteers for you and only ever trims power. Throttle is part of it: full power holds the angle, easing off lets the car come back straight. Handbrake (Space) puts the clutch in like a drifter would, so it locks the rear even with the throttle held; let go with the revs up for a clutch kick. Donuts: from walking pace, full lock and full throttle, and the assist steps aside; ease off the lock or the throttle and it catches the car and straightens it. Burnouts: TCS off (T) or Simulation (3).

Drift setup

Garage settings, not aids: the same physics with more steering lock (an angle kit; stock cars have ~32°, so drifts past ~35° can't be caught) and a different rear differential. A locked or tight LSD keeps both rear wheels spinning together, which holds a drift on the throttle but pushes on corner entry. Best with Simulation (3) or Drift (4), mouse steering and the Drift camera (C).

Garage

A design test bed, not a menu of cheats. Set camber, toe, caster and camber gain; springs, ride height, slow and fast dampers and anti-roll bars per axle; tyre compound (off-road to racing slick, rain) and pressure; brake balance; final drive; splitter, rear body downforce and a rear wing that really stalls; altitude, air temperature and wind. The HUD shows each tyre's tread temperature and camber, plus the downforce and drag. Slicks are slow until they are warm (or use tyre warmers), and too little brake balance spins the car. Setups are saved per car and can be shared as .json files.

Showcases

Physics overlay (O): each tyre's friction circle (how much force it can give at its load) and the force it gives now, the loads, the g vector, the roll centres and the roll axis. Rewind: hold Backspace; let go and you carry on from that moment (the physics is deterministic, so the replay is exact). Ghost (N): your last run from the same start drives beside you. Shadow car (J): a second car driven by your exact inputs, the stock version of yours or another model, through nothing: your setup against stock, same hands. Rain (I): wet grip, a water film, slick paint and cobbles, and puddles that can make a tyre hydroplane (water deeper than the tread clears, near 0.5-1 × the hydroplaning speed, Horne's 6.35 √p km/h) and pull the car toward them. Share link: this car, its setup, the weather and the place.

Moose test

ISO 3888-2 double lane change, the lane sized to your car's width. Drive in at a steady speed and lift off at the yellow line, or let the robot test driver do it (pure-pursuit steering, throttle released at the gate as the standard asks). The ladder raises the speed 4 km/h a run until a cone falls or the car rolls. Results are kept per car: try the 911 against the 4.2 t S 600 Guard.

Crash lab

The car goes on a sled at the test speed into the rigid barrier or, for the side test, the pole, on its full node/beam crash structure whatever the Damage setting. Every 1/360 s step is recorded, the structure's shape with it, and the impact replays at a tenth of real speed: the nose folds as the car stops. Then the report: chest, HIC15, head, excursion and injury risk per seat, the crush and any cabin intrusion, and the pulse, measured from the car's own deceleration (the dummies ride exactly that). Leave the lab and your Damage setting comes back. F repairs.

Stunt park

North of the smash lanes (Go to: Stunt). Two kickers (1.5 m and 3 m) with landing ramps, a half-pipe with 50° walls and parapets, and a 36 m clothoid loop that gets a road car over the top at ~100 km/h with about 4 g of centripetal acceleration at the bottom (a circular loop needs over 6 g); with gravity and the suspension settling, the toast's felt peak reads higher. Airtime, jump length and landing g after every jump. The loop is drawn on the inside of a cylinder and shifts sideways so the way in and out pass side by side; the assist steers the small corrections to stay on it.

Damage

Cosmetic is the game setting: hits leave shallow dents (a few centimetres, never folding the body in), there is no injury report, a car left on its side or roof is put back on its wheels by itself, and the car always drives the same. Realistic is the full model: the car's body is a node/beam crash structure (about 110 nodes, 380 elastic-plastic beams that yield, harden and tear) solved with the physics, so the nose crushes as it soaks up the hit and the car decelerates over the crush (about 0.5 m and 27 g into a rigid wall at 56 km/h), the roof caves in a rollover, a pole bites into a door. And parts break: a hard corner hit bends the suspension (the car pulls), tears a wheel off, pinches a tyre flat on a kerb; a crushed radiator leaks, the engine runs hot, loses power and seizes; a bent rack steers off-centre. The panel on the left lists what is broken. F repairs; Go to gives you a new car.

Crash dummies

Speed doesn't hurt; suddenly stopping does. Each hit's speed change becomes the crash pulse a crumple zone would give (front ~0.9 m of crush, sides ~0.3 m), and each dummy (torso + head on a neck, belt, airbags, door, wheel and dash) rides it out. The report gives chest g (3 ms), the Head Injury Criterion (HIC15) and the risk of life-threatening injury from NHTSA's published risk curves, plus a delta-V fatality statistic (Joksch). A game estimate that ranks crashes sensibly, not a validated crash test. Try the Crash barrier on the test track (Go to), belted and unbelted. The Smash lanes next to it end in walls that give way: a glass storefront breaks at a walking pace and barely slows you, a hollow-block wall holds below ~20 km/h and is breached above it, a double-brick wall needs ~45 km/h. Each is three sections, so you punch a hole and leave the rest; a wall that holds is weakened, and ramming again brings it down. The dummies feel only the speed you actually lose. R rebuilds the walls.

Two-wheelers

Pick the bicycle, e-scooter or motorcycle above. By default the steer input asks for a lean angle and the virtual rider finds the handlebar torque, counter-steer included: to go left at speed the bars first turn right, watch the steering bar. Bar-torque steering (L) gives you the handlebar directly: push left to lean right. Hard, real, best with mouse steering. Brake = both brakes, handbrake (Space) = rear brake only. Presets: Fun = ABS, traction and wheelie control; Sport = ABS; Simulation = nothing; 4 = wheelie assist (hold the throttle, the front stays up). Full throttle on the motorcycle without it loops the bike. The tyres lean (camber thrust, rounded crown); there's no suspension or gearbox yet, and a crash stops the ride for 2 s.

Telemetry

Records every frame: speed, g-forces, inputs, rpm and per-wheel load and slip.

Mouse steering

Click the view to grab the mouse: move left/right to turn the wheel, left button = throttle, right button = brake, middle button or Z centres the wheel, Esc lets go. Best with Simulation (3) for realistic, unfiltered steering.
