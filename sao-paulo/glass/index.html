Odds of breaking

No two panes are alike: each has its own scattering of microscopic flaws (a Weibull population, the same model the lab uses for every shot). So the same shot breaks one pane and not the next. This fires it into ten panes at each of ten speeds, every pane with fresh flaws, at the lab's physics, on copies of the engine running in the background.

Glass Lab

Every crack you see is computed, not animated. The page runs Glass Dynamics, a C++ glazing fracture engine compiled to WebAssembly; the page only draws what it returns. It is the same core that couples to the São Paulo World vehicle and flight engines (windscreens, side glass, aircraft windshields).

Controls

  • Tap or click the glass to fire the selected object there; Space fires at the aim point.
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom. C cycles the camera.
  • Playback runs from real time down to 1/100,000: one second on screen is ten microseconds of glass. Bullet time slows down by itself as the object arrives and the cracks run, then speeds back up for the falling pieces.
  • Sound (M) is synthesized from what the engine reports: each glass has its own voice (annealed cracks and crashes, tempered bursts into a hiss and rains dice, laminate crunches, armour thumps), and in bullet time everything drops in pitch and stretches out.
  • + Add a pane behind (under the glass list) stacks up to four panes of any glass with the gap you choose; the object and the flying shards of each pane hit the next one for real.
  • Sound plays a steady note at the glass. Every pane has natural notes (its plate modes); on one of them the swing builds up cycle after cycle until it breaks. Sing lets your voice drive it, boosted to what a loudspeaker could do. T plays or stops the note.
  • Prince Rupert's drop (in Scenarios) opens its own page: shoot its head, snap its tail, or see its frozen stress through crossed polarisers.
  • Glass cutter and Circle cutter score the glass (drag a line, or tap for a circle); Knock it out taps it from behind with a rubber mallet, and the crack follows the score.
  • Odds of breaking at every speed (under Fire) fires the object into a hundred panes, each with its own flaws, and plots how often they break. Certification scenarios (ECE R43, ASTM E1996, the FAA bird strike, ANSI Z97.1, UL 752) say whether the glass passed.
  • Read the cracks is a forensics puzzle: which of three stones hit first? A window that explodes on its own breaks a tempered panel with a nickel-sulfide stone.
  • Share copies a link to exactly this setup and shot: whoever opens it sees the same glass, the same shot and the same cracks.
  • S shows the bending stress as a photoelastic fringe pattern, R fits a new pane, Z replays the last shot exactly (the fracture is deterministic).

What is solved

  • Plate dynamics: the layup becomes one Kirchhoff plate (shear coupling of the interlayers by the Bennison-Wölfel effective-thickness method), solved by modal superposition with exact time steps from 50 ns to milliseconds, plus the logarithmic stress peak under a concentrated load that the modes cannot hold.
  • Contact: Hertz with Thornton plasticity and Hunt-Crossley damping for balls, cone indentation at the glass hardness for sharp tips, a flat punch that crushes (timber, brick), Wilbeck's shock and steady-flow pressures for the bird.
  • Strength: Weibull flaw populations over each face and edge, sped up by the stress-corrosion rate law (a microsecond impact finds glass about twice as strong as a minute-long load). Tempered and chemically strengthened plies carry their residual stress profile.
  • Cracks: through-cracks grow tip by tip at up to the terminal crack speed (~1,500 m/s), turn by the maximum hoop-stress kink criterion, fork where stress times the square root of the run passes the branching constant (the fracture-mirror relation), shield and arrest each other, and stop at existing cracks. Radial cracks, concentric rings, Hertzian cones, crush zones and tempered dicing all come out of that.
  • Laminates and armour: broken plies hand the load to the interlayer as an axisymmetric membrane that tears at its rim; bullets crush a cone through each ply (the white crater), mushroom if soft and are caught (or not) by the layers behind, calibrated against UL 752 ratings. A round off square meets each layer at its slant thickness and glances off near grazing; one landing in an old crater only has to push the rubble aside. A shotgun shell (00 buckshot) fires its nine pellets as separate projectiles across its pattern, arriving one after another.
  • Pieces: enclosed regions are cut out as polygons and flown as rigid bodies (gravity, drag, floor friction); hard landings break them again. A shattered tempered pane is a sheet of dice held only by friction: it lets go from the hole up, in slabs that come apart in the air, and the frame keeps a ragged fringe. Each piece is drawn as its real dice (cells of the real fragment size, extruded to the glass thickness), and the dice you see in the crazed pane are the very ones that fall, keeping their shapes, some still stuck together in small clumps; in bullet time every die flies, bounces and slides on its own path.

Armoured V8 under fire

The first scenario puts you in the driver's seat of the Rosser V8 (the car from the driving sim) with a 32 mm armoured windscreen while five gunmen fire about 190 rounds of 9 mm in ten seconds. The screen is raked about 70 degrees, so the rounds strike it at a glancing angle: they see a much thicker barrier, and some skip off. Every round that reaches the glass is fired into the engine with its real angle and arrival time; the craters, spider webs and the driver's view all come from the physics, and nothing is pre-scripted except where the gunmen aim. C switches between the driver, the outside and a gunman; Replay shows it again at any speed.

Grenade in the shop

A glass shop on a street at night (Day / Night switches): a storefront of four floor-to-ceiling panels of 10 mm fully tempered glass, a frameless glass door with a transom over it, and three 6 mm tempered windows on every other wall, each pane its own engine pane. Tap a window (or press Throw) and a crash-test dummy throws a grenade through it: the throw plays in real time and only slows as the grenade reaches the glass, which the engine breaks for real. It rattles round the room and goes off 1.6 s after the throw. Its steel reaches the panes first, then the blast wave: each pane gets the pressure, duration and arrival time its own distance and angle from the charge give, plus the gas of a closed room venting through its windows. The panel at the bottom says what reaches the dummy: the blast (eardrum and lung injury), the steel (against the 79 J incapacitation mark) and the flying glass. C switches views; Replay throws the same throw again; Share sends a link straight to the scene. The Glass menu reglazes the shop: tempered, annealed, laminated, hurricane or bullet-resistant. Where the glass would stop a thrown grenade, the door is propped open to throw through. Panes far away are drawn with less detail, and panes behind a wall not at all, so it runs lighter.

The full working

Every equation, constant and source is on the How it works page, with calculators for the impact energy and the blast.

Runs on anything

The physics is the same on every device: the same pane breaks the same way on a phone and on a desktop. The Auto quality setting benchmarks this device and picks how to draw it: resolution, shadows, materials and how many of the real dice are shown. Where the browser allows, the physics runs on a core of its own. If the device can't keep up, the clock slows down instead of skipping physics (the bar shows "device-limited"). Without WebGL the lab draws a flat front view.

Honest limits

Panes are flat and simply supported. Layups for bullet-resistant and aircraft glazing are representative builds, not certified products, and the ballistic constants are fitted to UL 752 ratings. The engine flies tempered glass as pieces somewhat larger than its real dice, and the page cuts each into dice of the real size as it comes apart (fewer of them on lower drawing settings); the result panel gives the real fragment size from the energy balance.
