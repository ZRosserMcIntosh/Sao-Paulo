      Under the hood

      One map, many minds

      The lane map

      Every street in the game is broken into lanes with a direction, a width, a speed limit and the turns allowed from it. Lane 0 is always the leftmost lane in the direction of travel, and Brazil drives on the right. Signals, PARE signs, roundabouts, bus lanes, crosswalks and bus stops all hang off the same map, so nothing has to be guessed from the 3D models.

      One route planner

      The GPS, normal traffic, the pursuing parties and fleeing drivers all ask the same route planner for directions. The only difference is the rules they're told to follow: the GPS obeys everything, a pursuing party may ignore signals and bus lanes, and a panicking driver may break almost any rule to get away.

      Seeing and hearing

      Big events (an explosion, shots, a crash) are heard within a radius. People inside it react in their own way: pedestrians run, drivers stop or flee, pursuing parties note where it happened. Panic spreads to anyone who sees someone running, then fades.

      Near and far

      In the game, only the traffic close to you runs full car physics. Further out, cars glide along their lanes with the same rules but much less work, and beyond that the city just keeps count of how busy each street is. That's how a whole city of traffic fits in a normal PC.

      Safe by default

      In normal traffic, drivers always stop for pedestrians. Contact can only happen in chaos, during a pursuit or a panic, and then it's a fall: the person gets up and limps away. Motorcycle falls are rare, a few per hour of play, and clear quickly.

      What this page simplifies

      This browser version draws the city flat, uses a simple driving model for every car, builds the sidewalks from the road outlines (the game will use a dedicated pedestrian map), and only shows one 300–400 m piece at a time. The rules are the same.
