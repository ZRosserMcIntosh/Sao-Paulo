São Paulo WorldFlight test bed

Aircraft

The Top G flies on the G650ER's published figures: two 75 kN turbofans with thrust lapse and spool lag, a swept supercritical wing with Mach drag rise, Fowler flaps, speedbrakes and retractable gear. Mmo 0.925, Vmo 340 kt.

Start

Flying

Assists only move the controls, as a skilled pilot would: Easy levels the wings, coordinates turns, trims and guards the stall; Simulation is the raw airplane. Lift, thrust and weight are identical in all three.

Sound

Synthesized from the simulation: firing pulses at the engine rpm, louder with manifold pressure, wind from the airspeed, and the stall-warning reed horn.

Synthesized from the simulation: fan tone and core whine from each engine's N1 and N2, roar with thrust, wind from the airspeed, and the stall warning.

Failures

Failures act on the physical systems: with the fuel off the engine starves after the line fuel is used; a failed vacuum pump lets the gyros spin down; a blocked pitot bleeds the airspeed to zero.

Take-off: full throttle, keep it straight with Q/E, ease back at 55 knots. Climb at 75. Land at 65 with flaps down, idle over the runway, flare gently.

Physics: the Aviation Dynamics core (the C++ the game will use, github.com/ZRosserMcIntosh/Aviation-Dynamics) compiled to WebAssembly, stepping at 240 Hz: strip-theory wings, a blade-element propeller and piston engine (172) or two-spool turbofans (Top G), fuel, electrics and instruments; the 172 is validated against its published handbook performance, the Top G against the G650ER's published figures. The ground is flat at Campo de Marte's elevation (722 m) under the São Paulo World map; real terrain and buildings arrive with the world tile export.
