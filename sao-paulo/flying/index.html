Aircraft

The Top G flies on the G650ER's published figures: two 75 kN turbofans with thrust lapse and spool lag, a swept supercritical wing with Mach drag rise, Fowler flaps, speedbrakes and retractable gear. Mmo 0.925, Vmo 340 kt.

Destination

Any of Brazil's 1,150 airports with a paved runway: type part of its name, its city or its code (ICAO or IATA; accents and small typos are fine). Pick a runway: NAV 1 is tuned to its ILS (where it has one) with the course set, and the GPS loads a route to it — from the runway if you are on it, otherwise from where you are — down to a 3° path to the threshold. Two starts below put you on its approach. Runways from OurAirports; ILS identifiers and frequencies from published listings (only some airports'); antenna sites estimated. Not for real-world navigation.

Wind and weather

The wind is the airfield report: it grows and backs with height and brings its own gusts over the city. Turbulence follows MIL-HDBK-1797; a wet runway brakes like the certification wet-runway curve, and in standing water the tyres hydroplane above 9·√(tyre psi) kt (about 124 kt for the Top G, 55 kt for the 172).

Take-off in the 172: full throttle, keep it straight with Q/E, ease back at 55 knots. Climb at 75. Land at 65 with flaps down, idle over the runway, flare gently.

Flying

Assists only move the controls, as a skilled pilot would: Easy levels the wings, coordinates turns, trims and guards the stall; Simulation is the raw airplane. Lift, thrust and weight are identical in all three. Auto-trim (any mode): let go of the stick and it levels the wings, stops the climb or descent, then holds that heading and altitude and trims for you; near the stall it gives up height rather than speed. It never touches the throttle.

Display

The instruments stay in knots, as in the airplane; the readouts add km/h or mph.

Sound

Synthesized from the simulation: firing pulses at the engine rpm, louder with manifold pressure, wind from the airspeed, and the stall-warning reed horn.

Synthesized from the simulation: fan tone and core whine from each engine's N1 and N2, roar with thrust, wind from the airspeed, and the stall warning.

Failures

Failures act on the physical systems: with the fuel off the engine starves after the line fuel is used; a failed vacuum pump lets the gyros spin down; a blocked pitot bleeds the airspeed to zero; with both generators off the battery carries the bus until it is flat.

About

Physics: the Aviation Dynamics core (the C++ the game will use, github.com/ZRosserMcIntosh/Aviation-Dynamics) compiled to WebAssembly, stepping at 240 Hz: strip-theory wings, a blade-element propeller and piston engine (172) or two-spool turbofans (Top G), fuel, electrics and instruments; the 172 is validated against its published handbook performance, the Top G against the G650ER's published figures. Every airport of the region sits at its own elevation (Congonhas 802 m, Campo de Marte 722 m, Guarulhos 750 m, Viracopos 661 m, São José dos Campos 646 m, Jundiaí 753 m), the ground blended smoothly between them under the São Paulo World map; real terrain and buildings arrive with the world tile export.
