São Paulo WorldFlight test bed

Aircraft

The King Air 350: two 1,050 shp PT6A-60A turboprops (torque and ITT limited at the power levers' forward stop), four-blade constant-speed propellers governed from 1,450 to 1,700 rpm, with feather, ground beta and reverse, autofeather, a T-tail and winglets. Power levers on the throttle keys, propeller levers ⇧, ⇧., condition levers ⇧1 ⇧2 ⇧3, beta ⇧N and reverse N on the ground.

The Top G flies on the G650ER's published figures: two 75 kN turbofans with thrust lapse and spool lag, a swept supercritical wing with Mach drag rise, Fowler flaps, speedbrakes and retractable gear. Mmo 0.925, Vmo 340 kt.

Destination

Any of Brazil's 1,150 airports with a paved runway: type part of its name, its city or its code (ICAO or IATA; accents and small typos are fine). Pick a runway: NAV 1 is tuned to its ILS (where it has one) with the course set, and the GPS loads a route to it — from the runway if you are on it, otherwise from where you are — down to a 3° path to the threshold. Two starts below put you on its approach. Runways from OurAirports; ILS identifiers and frequencies from published listings (only some airports'); antenna sites estimated. Not for real-world navigation.

Wind and weather

Rain, ice and fronts

This aircraft has no ice protection to switch (its pitot heat is on the electrical panel).

—

Rain falls from the cloud's top to the ground: the visibility falls with it, and the runways wet over a few minutes (a water film, standing water in a downpour) and dry after. Icing puts a supercooled stratus 1,200 m deep round the altitude you are at when you choose it (choose again to move it): in it the airframe ices, the stall speed rises and the windshield frosts; below 0 °C rain is freezing rain. The jets' ice detector shows ICE DETECTED; their wing (and the CJ4's tail) anti-ice or boots are the buttons above, or \ and ⇧\. The 172 can ice its carburettor in humid air even when warm: the rpm sags, and carb heat (\) melts it. A front moves across the field at the minute set: behind a cold front it is 6 °C colder, the QNH 3 hPa higher and the wind 15 kt from 200°; behind a warm front 4 °C warmer, 2 hPa lower, 10 kt from 320°. An altimeter set ahead of it reads wrong behind it: the ATIS line has the field's QNH.

The wind is the airfield report: it grows and backs with height and brings its own gusts over the city. Turbulence follows MIL-HDBK-1797; a wet runway brakes like the certification wet-runway curve, and in standing water the tyres hydroplane above 9·√(tyre psi) kt (about 124 kt for the Top G, 55 kt for the 172).

Flying

ALT— VS— SPD— DA—

Terrain awareness (TAWS: class A in the jets, B in the others) watches the region's terrain and buildings ahead and below: CAUTION TERRAIN, then TERRAIN AHEAD, PULL UP, and the GPWS calls (SINK RATE, DON'T SINK, TOO LOW GEAR / FLAPS, GLIDESLOPE, BANK ANGLE, the height callouts). The alert shows on the PFD (red PULL UP, amber TERRAIN), as a tag on the analog 172, and is spoken with the sound on (M). TERR INHIB turns the forward-looking terrain alerts off for flying low near hills on purpose; the GPWS modes stay. DH: the decision height on the radio altimeter, where MINIMUMS is called; the radio altitude shows on the PFD below 2,500 ft.

Prompts (Easy and Sport): when something goes wrong — carburettor or airframe ice, the pitot in freezing cloud, an engine out, the gear still up close to the ground, an overspeed, a generator or the alternator off line, a fuel tank running low — a box under the TAWS tag says what is happening, the number that shows it, and what to do: F (or a tap) does it with the cockpit's own switches and levers, exactly as if you had found them; Esc or ✕ puts it away for 90 s. Warnings (red) come before cautions (amber) and advisories (blue), one at a time, spoken once with the sound on. Simulation has none: there it is the annunciators, the EICAS and the TAWS.

Assists only move the controls, as a skilled pilot would: Easy levels the wings, coordinates turns, trims and guards the stall; Simulation is the raw airplane. Lift, thrust and weight are identical in all three. Auto-trim (any mode): let go of the stick and it levels the wings, stops the climb or descent, then holds that heading and altitude and trims for you; near the stall it gives up height rather than speed. It never touches the throttle.

Autopilot (in the air, Y): the flight core's autopilot, flown from the instruments. Y engages it holding the present heading (HDG, on the heading bug: ; and ' turn it), altitude (ALT) and, on the jets, speed (SPD, the autothrottle). NAV follows the GPS route to the destination; VS climbs or descends at the selected rate to the selected altitude and levels off there (PgUp and PgDn move it by 500 ft; changing it starts the climb or descent). It runs the trim, banks at most 25°, and lets go on the ground; the stick or Y gives you the controls back.

APR (⇧Y), the approach mode: ILS flies the heading bug to intercept the localizer on NAV 1 (set the bug to the intercept heading), captures it inside full scale (HDG | LOC armed, then LOC | GS armed), then the glideslope from level or below within a quarter dot (LOC | GS), and flies both down to the DA; GPS does the same on the GPS approach to the destination (LNAV, then LPV on its glidepath). At the DA the autopilot lets go: MINIMUMS — AP DISCONNECT flashes with a tone and you land the airplane; if SPD was on, the autothrottle stays in until you take it out (SPD or Y) for the flare. DA: the decision altitude on the altimeter, by default the destination's field elevation + 200 ft. Modes on the status line and the PFDs: active green, armed white.

Autoland (in the air, U): the blind-landing pilot of the flight core flies the GPS approach to the destination (the runway in use if none is picked) from the instruments alone. It joins the final where it can fly the 3° path from there, else it flies the whole procedure (the initial fix, the intermediate fix, level at the final approach fix's altitude); it lowers the gear, puts the flaps out as the speed allows, sets the altimeter to QNH, flares, lands and brakes to a stop on the centreline. Moving the stick, or U, gives you the controls back.

Display

172 panel

The six-pack, or the glass cockpit of a 172S NAV III: the PFD (attitude, speed and altitude tapes, HSI on NAV 1 or the GPS) and the MFD (moving map over the region's terrain, the engine indication system). Scroll the HDG, CRS, barometer and selected-altitude boxes or the map to turn their knobs; the softkeys click.

The instruments stay in knots, as in the airplane; the readouts add km/h or mph.

The city's buildings from OpenStreetMap, at their mapped heights, and where OpenStreetMap has none the footprints traced from imagery by Google and Microsoft, their heights estimated from the mapped buildings around them: every building within a few kilometres, the skyline (35 m and taller) beyond. Graphics

—

Buildings in detail: every building within this distance of the aircraft (a little farther as you climb); beyond it the skyline, the buildings 35 m and taller, out to its distance. View distance: how far the haze lets you see, and the ground drawn to it. Shadows: the aircraft's shadow on the ground and the roofs near it, everywhere, or none. Resolution: the most pixels per screen pixel; it drops by itself when frames run slow. Low suits phones and older laptops; Medium most computers; High and Ultra a fast graphics card.

Synthesized from the simulation: each turbine's whine from its Ng, each four-blade propeller's tone at the blade-passing frequency (4 × Np), buzzier with torque, the roar of beta and reverse, wind from the airspeed, and the stall warning.

Failures

King Air: Engine 1 cut-off moves its condition lever to CUT-OFF; the propeller windmills on its governor (drag) until it is feathered (Feather 1), or the armed autofeather does it.

Failures act on the physical systems: with the fuel off the engine starves after the line fuel is used; a failed vacuum pump lets the gyros spin down; a blocked pitot bleeds the airspeed to zero; with both generators off the battery carries the bus until it is flat.

About

Physics: the Aviation Dynamics core (the C++ the game will use, github.com/ZRosserMcIntosh/Aviation-Dynamics) compiled to WebAssembly, stepping at 240 Hz: strip-theory wings, a blade-element propeller and piston engine (172) or two-spool turbofans (Top G), fuel, electrics and instruments; the 172 is validated against its published handbook performance, the Top G against the G650ER's published figures. Every airport of the region sits at its own elevation (Congonhas 802 m, Campo de Marte 722 m, Guarulhos 750 m, Viracopos 661 m, São José dos Campos 646 m, Jundiaí 753 m), the ground blended smoothly between them under the São Paulo World map; real terrain and buildings arrive with the world tile export.
