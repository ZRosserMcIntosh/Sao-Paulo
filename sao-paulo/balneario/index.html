Checkpoint C

Every building, with a flag

Balneário publishes a pseudo-3D cadastre: each building unit has a base level (cota) and a height (pe_direito), so podiums and towers come out as stacked parts. Checked against OpenStreetMap floor counts it agrees to a median ratio of 1.0. Where the cadastre was never surveyed the height comes from OSM, from a named-tower list (CTBUH), or from a flagged guess.

Height sources

Colours are the same in Blender: each confidence class is a material, so a provisional guess is never mistaken for a surveyed height. Praia Brava is in Itajaí, whose cadastre gives floor counts rather than heights.

Street names & CEPs

Rua 3700, CEP 88330-203

Balneário names its cross streets by number and numbers its houses by the metre: a house at 450 m from Avenida Atlântica is number 450, and Rua 2400 crosses the avenue at number 2400. The Correios ranges confirm it, so every piece of every street carries an estimated number range and the CEP that covers it.

  1. Names. OpenStreetMap ways are matched to the city's official street axes within 15 m; unnamed service roads take the official name. Praia Brava uses Itajaí's lot register.
  2. CEPs. One ViaCEP reverse search per street (Correios data), with the complemento ranges parsed into number intervals and sides.
  3. Numbers. Metric distance along the official axis from the origin end. The origin is fitted to known house numbers where there are any (OSM addresses, Itajaí lots); otherwise cross streets start at Avenida Atlântica and shore-parallel streets at their north end. Every unanchored range is flagged as an estimate.
  4. In the game. The same index feeds the blue corner plates and the street banner, as in São Paulo.
