How the Street Lab works

Two of the game's native C++ modules run here, compiled to WebAssembly: Pedestrian Dynamics for people and Street Dynamics for signs, posts, bollards and bike racks. The page drives the car and draws what they compute. The same code gives the same results, bit for bit, in the Unity build ("Replay & compare" shows the state hash).

The person

  • The body. Sixteen segments with the masses of de Leva's anthropometric tables and the lengths of Drillis and Contini, scaled to the height and weight you pick. The joints have the ranges of the standard clinical tables (hip 120° forward and 20° back, knee 0–140°…), and past them the ligaments tear.
  • The muscles. Until the car arrives the person stands, holding the pose. A car striking faster than walking pace takes the muscles away, as it does in real collisions.
  • The car's front. Bumper, bonnet edge, bonnet, cowl, windscreen, pillars and roof, each with its own stiffness. A sedan's bonnet edge is below an adult's centre of mass: the body wraps onto the bonnet. An SUV's or a van's front is not: the body is thrown ahead (forward projection). A child in front of a sedan is projected too.
  • The numbers are the ones a crash lab reads: HIC15 for the head (above 1,000 is serious), upper-tibia acceleration and knee bending (the legform limits are 150 g and 15°), the throw distance against Searle's bounds, and the kinematics class (Ravani et al.).

The street furniture

  • The car is the page's own simple rigid body: its mass, brakes and tyres, and a front that crushes past about 80 kN (stiffening as it goes). It takes back every impulse the person and the objects put on it.
  • A 2½" galvanised post folds where it is hit and the sign plate tears off its clamps; a cast-iron frade snaps; a concrete-filled steel bollard bends a little and stops the car; a sign on a breakaway slip base lets go and leaves a 5 cm stub. The posts' strengths come from their sections and the steel's yield, following EN 1993.

What it does not do yet

The person does not step to catch their balance or brace with their arms; that comes next. Loose plates and posts do not hit the person; the car's front does not dent. Some figures are estimates: the modules' READMEs list every source and what is still being calibrated (sideways knee bending reads high; the van's flat front throws people too far).

A game simulation, not a safety tool.
