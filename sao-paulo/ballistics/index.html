Ballistics Range

Every round here is flown, not animated. The page runs Ballistic Dynamics, a C++ exterior-ballistics engine compiled to WebAssembly, through air from the Aviation Dynamics standard atmosphere; it only draws what the engine returns.

How to shoot

  • Pick a scenario, or set the load, the distance, the air and the wind yourself, and press Fire (Space).
  • Your dope (the elevation and wind holds) is solved for the wind you call. The round flies through the true wind, which can change along the range and gusts. Untick "Call the average true wind exactly" to call it yourself, the way a shooter reads the flags and the mirage.
  • Bullet time (B) follows the round from the muzzle to the target, slowing right down on the way in. The slow motion is the same flight: the engine's steps never change, only how fast they are shown.
  • Cameras (C): the scope with its MIL reticle and the mirage, the shooter's shoulder, the bullet, and the target.
  • Share copies a link that rebuilds exactly this shot: the same air, the same gusts at the same instant, the same impact.
  • Trace (T) shows the round's wake bending the light as it goes downrange, the swirl a spotter watches through the scope. Hit markers: an X flash on a hit, and marks on the reticle where your last shots went (every impact, misses too, if you choose).
  • Fire again while a round is still flying: several can be in the air at once. A bolt action takes about a second to work; a semi-automatic fires as fast as you press. In bullet time the rounds still leave at the rifle's real pace.
  • Scope: put any scope on any rifle: mil-dot, MOA, the ACOG 4×32, the PSO-1 or the PU. Aim: Assisted sets your dope for you; Manual hands you the turrets (R/F elevation, Q/E windage) and the crosshair (drag in the scope or use the arrow keys). Dial the dope sets the elevation from your card; the wind is yours.
  • Tracer: light the round in red, green, orange, yellow or white; it stays lit to the impact, or untick that to have it burn out at its real range. The Mosin M91/30 and the SVD wear their own scopes, the PU and the PSO-1: set the drum to the hundreds of metres the dope card gives, then hold the rest in thousandths (the red ring).
  • The armoured V8: the Rosser V8 from the driving sim with a crash dummy at the wheel behind its armoured windscreen, raked about 70°. The Glass engine takes the round at its real angle: it may stop it, glance off, or let it through to the driver, and the Liquids Lab plays the rest.
  • Set the target to Crash dummy behind glass (or pick Through a windscreen): a hit opens the impact in the Liquids Lab, with the round arriving at the speed and angle this range computed; the Glass engine breaks the pane and the fluid engine does the rest, in bullet time.

What is solved

  • Drag: the standard G7 or G1 drag curve scaled by each bullet's published ballistic coefficient, against the Mach number in the local air.
  • Air: the U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 with your temperature, altitude and humidity (moist air is lighter): density, speed of sound and density altitude change along the path.
  • Wind: up to three stretches along the range, each with its own speed, direction and gusts, blended at the joins.
  • Spin: Miller's stability from the twist, the yaw of repose and the spin drift it gives, the aerodynamic jump from a crosswind, the wobble after the muzzle, and tumbling if the twist is too slow.
  • The Earth: Coriolis (right in the northern hemisphere, left in the southern) and Eötvös (a round fired east rises, one fired west drops).

Honest limits

Every round of a load is the same round: muzzle-velocity spread, powder temperature and group size come in the next milestone, so the temperature here changes the air, not the powder. Spin-drift coefficients are estimated per bullet class and land within about 20 % of Litz's measured formula. Transonic dynamic instability is not modelled yet. The range is flat.
