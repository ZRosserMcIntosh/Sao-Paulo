Ballistic Dynamics · exterior ballistics

How the range works

Every round on the range is flown by Ballistic Dynamics, a C++ exterior-ballistics engine compiled to WebAssembly. The page only draws what it returns. This is the full working: the equations, where the numbers come from, how far each model has been checked, and what is still an estimate.

1Overview

A round is a state: its position and velocity in the world, its spin rate and roll angle. Every millisecond the engine advances that state with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta step through the forces on it: drag against the air it is in, gravity, the lift from its spin, and the rotating Earth. Nothing is looked up from a table of drops: a 2-mile shot is about 6,400 steps of the same equations.

dp/dt = v
dv/dt = −K(M) ρ |u| u + g + alift − 2 Ω × v,    u = v − w(p, t)
dspin/dt = ½ ρ |u| S d² Clp spin / Ix p position, v velocity, u velocity through the air (w the wind where the round is), ρ air density, M Mach number, g gravity, Ω the Earth's rotation. The step is fixed at 1 ms whatever your frame rate.

The source is open: github.com/ZRosserMcIntosh/ballistic-dynamics (MIT). The engine's own docs/physics.md and docs/validation.md carry everything below in more detail.

2The air

The air is not the ballistics engine's own: it is Aviation Dynamics' U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976, the same model that flies the aircraft on this site, compiled into the same WebAssembly module. Your temperature, altitude and humidity become a temperature offset from the standard day and a sea-level pressure whose profile passes through the station; the air then changes along the path as the round climbs and falls.

ρ = (p − e) / (Rd T) + e / (Rv T),   e = RH · es(T)   (Buck 1996)
a = √(γ Rd Tv),   Tv = p / (ρ Rd) Moist air is lighter than dry air at the same pressure and temperature (water vapour is lighter than nitrogen), so humidity lowers drag a little and raises the speed of sound. Density altitude is the standard-day altitude with the same density.

Why it matters: drag is proportional to density. A hot, high, thin day (the 2-mile scenario: 32 °C at 1,500 m, a density altitude near 2,450 m) lets the round keep its speed; a cold morning at sea level does the opposite. Compare Cold morning and Hot afternoon: the same rifle drops about a metre more at 1 km in the cold.

3Drag

Each bullet's drag comes from a published ballistic coefficient scaling a standard drag curve: G7 (a long boat-tail, the shape of modern match bullets) where the maker or Bryan Litz publishes one, G1 otherwise. Both curves are the U.S. Army Ballistic Research Laboratory's, drag coefficient against Mach number from 0 to 5.

BC = m / (i d²)     adrag = ρ u² CD,ref(M) π / (8 BC) i is the form factor: how much draggier the bullet is than the reference shape. Published BCs in lb/in² are multiplied by 703.07 for kg/m². Sierra's stepped BCs (a different value per speed band) are applied as published.

The curve's tall hump is the transonic zone: drag about triples between Mach 0.8 and 1.2. Rounds that go subsonic before the target (the result panel says where) slow less afterwards but drift more in the wind.

4Wind along the range

The true wind is laid along the range in up to three stretches (near, middle, far third), each with a speed, a direction and gusts: a smooth, seeded variation of the speed and a wander of the direction around them, blended over 20 m at each join so the round never sees a step. The flags show it live: their angle is about 4° per mph from the pole.

The drift a wind gives is not proportional to where it blows: a crosswind near the muzzle turns the round when it has the whole flight ahead of it, so the near wind counts most. That is why calling the average wind misses in Three winds.

The mirage in the scope is drawn from the same air: it boils harder in heat and still air, and leans and runs with the crosswind, the way shooters read it.

5Spin and stability

The rifling spins the round at 2π V / twist: a .308 at 2,600 fps from the M24's 1:11.25 in barrel turns 2,773 times a second. Spin keeps a long bullet pointed forward. How well is the gyroscopic stability factor Sg, from Don Miller's rule with its velocity, temperature and pressure corrections:

Sg = 30 m / (t² d³ l (1 + l²)) · (V / 2800)1/3 · (TF + 460) / 519 · 29.92 / P m grains, t twist in calibres per turn, d diameter in inches, l length in calibres, V fps, T °F, P inHg. Below 1 the round is unstable and tumbles; 1 to 1.4 is marginal; above 1.5 is safe. Downrange Sg follows Sg ∝ spin² / (ρ V² Cmα): it grows as the round slows.

6Spin drift

A spinning round whose path curves under gravity does not point straight along it: like a gyroscope it settles with its nose slightly to one side, the yaw of repose (McCoy, Modern Exterior Ballistics, ch. 10). The air pushes on that yaw, and the round drifts: to the right for a right-hand twist. The engine computes it from the physics, not a formula:

αR = −(4 (Iy/Ix) Sg / spin) · (u × a) / |u|²     alift = ½ ρ |u|² S CLα αR / m The overturning moment is taken from Sg itself, so only the inertia ratio Iy/Ix (from the bullet's length) and the lift slope CLα are needed. Both are estimated for the bullet class.

Checked against Bryan Litz's formula from measured data, 1.25 (Sg + 1.2) TOF1.83 inches: the engine lands at 0.79 to 0.98 of it for the five rifle loads, without tuning (at 1,000 yd the .308 drifts 9.4 in against Litz's 11.2).

7Aerodynamic jump

A crosswind at the muzzle meets a round that is pointing along the bore: it starts out yawed into the wind, and its first swing turns the crosswind into a vertical kick. For a right-hand twist a wind from the left lifts the shot and a wind from the right drops it. The engine applies Litz's published regression:

Y = 0.01 Sg − 0.0024 l + 0.032   MOA per mph of crosswind

8The wobble

Leaving the muzzle, the nose circles the path in two superposed motions, a fast nutation and a slow precession, at frequencies set by the spin and Sg, ωf,s = (Ix spin / 2 Iy)(1 ± √(1 − 1/Sg)), starting from the crosswind yaw and damping out within a fraction of a second. In bullet time the round is drawn with it, together with the yaw of repose and its real spin (the rifling's engraving turns with it).

9Coriolis and Eötvös

The range turns with the Earth while the round is in the air. In the range's frame that is an acceleration −2 Ω × v, with the Earth's rotation Ω = ω (0, cos φ, sin φ) at latitude φ:

  • Coriolis: to the right in the northern hemisphere, to the left in the southern (São Paulo, 23.55° S), more toward the poles.
  • Eötvös: firing east the round is lifted, firing west it is pressed down, most at the equator. Try the Coriolis scenario and turn "Firing toward" from 90° to 270°.

With drag the sideways Coriolis drift is less than the textbook ω sin φ · X · T: drag damps the sideways velocity too, and the slow end of the flight weighs most. The engine's 3-D result matches the exact lateral equation to 0.3 % (a .50 BMG at 2,000 yd, 45° N: 10.2 in, not 11.5).

10Zero, dope and the call

Every rifle is zeroed at 100 m: the engine finds the bore angle that crosses the line of sight there. Your dope is the elevation and wind hold for the target, solved by flying the round through the wind you call (and, if ticked, with spin and Coriolis). The elevation is dialled; the wind is held on the reticle (the red ring shows where to put the target). The shot is then fired through the true wind, with its gusts at that instant. The difference between the call and the truth is your miss.

11Bullet time

Slow motion never changes the physics. The engine flies the whole shot first, in its fixed 1 ms steps; the page then shows it at any speed. Between steps the round's state comes from a cubic Hermite interpolation of the step states (positions and velocities at both ends), within 20 µm of a run with steps twenty times finer, so a camera at 1/1,000 speed sees the round glide, never jump. In Bullet time the speed follows the round: slow out of the muzzle, faster down the range so a 6-second flight takes a few seconds, then down to about 1/2,500 for the last metres.

12Same shot, every machine

The engine's maths uses only correctly rounded operations: its own exp, log, sin, cos and atan (fdlibm's algorithms), no fused multiply-adds. The same inputs give the same bits on Linux, macOS, Windows and in every browser; continuous integration checks three golden hashes on all of them. That is why a Share link fires exactly the same shot for whoever opens it: the same gust at the same instant, the same impact to the micrometre.

14What is estimated

estimated The lift slope, the inertia ratio and the roll damping behind spin drift are typical values for the bullet class, not measured for each bullet; the bullet lengths too. The spin drift lands within about 20 % of Litz.
not yet Every round of a load is the same round: muzzle-velocity spread, powder temperature (the temperature here changes the air, not the powder), ballistic-coefficient spread and group size are the engine's next milestone. Transonic dynamic instability (a stable round starting to wobble as it goes subsonic) is not modelled; gyroscopic instability (too slow a twist) is. The range is flat and the Earth's curvature is left out (0.8 m at 3.2 km).
published Every number in the engine's data carries its source; manufacturers' figures came through the pages named in the validation table where the makers' own sites could not be reached from the build.
